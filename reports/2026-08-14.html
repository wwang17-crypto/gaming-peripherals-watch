Section 03 · Analyst notes

Today's takeaways

Friday's one card is the second Xbox controller story in three days, and unlike Wednesday's paint job this one is actual hardware. A redditor bought what looks like an Elite Series 3 engineering sample for $200 on OfferUp, posted the photographs to r/Controller on Aug 11, then deleted the account; the gallery came down fast enough that Windows Central floated an unconfirmed cease-and-desist, and the images survive mainly because Tom Warren re-shared them. Booked off PC Gamer's Aug 13 write-up, with Press Start's Aug 12 piece corroborating. Two things here that the May Anatel filing did not have: a display strip between the bumpers, and a Pair button that switches between Local Mode and Cloud Mode — a controller that talks to xCloud directly rather than through the console, which is the Stadia idea rebuilt on a platform that actually has the streaming estate to justify it. The rest is repairability and cost engineering: Torx T6 screws with a repair QR code in the battery bay, a user-replaceable 1,528 mAh pack replacing the Series 2's sealed 2,050 mAh, two scroll wheels, a flatter plate-style D-pad. Booked as a Leak, not a launch — the unit does not pair, the battery is stamped “PROTOTYPE NOT FOR SALE”, the regulatory fields are placeholders, and there is no price or date. Warren's sources say before year end; Gamescom is eleven days out. Worth noting against Wednesday's card: the complaint that Gears of War: E-Day shipped without an Elite variant now reads differently if the Elite line is mid-generation-change.

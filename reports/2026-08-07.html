Section 03 · Analyst notes

Today's takeaways

Friday breaks the pattern from the bottom of the market, not the top. CORSAIR's second announcement in three days is the mirror image of Wednesday's Trak Racer acquisition — where that one bought the whole simulator rig, this one goes after the £60 shelf. On Aug 5 CORSAIR launched the SKIFF 100 ($59.99) and SKIFF 100 PLUS ($69.99) membrane keyboards and the HARPOON v2 WIRELESS mouse ($69.99), all three shipping now in black with a two-year warranty. The pitch is explicitly "first-time gamers and people balancing work and play", and the spec sheets read accordingly: quiet membrane switches, a rotary media dial, 10-zone RGB, IP53 spill resistance and a detachable soft-touch wrist rest on the boards, with the PLUS adding six programmable G-keys inside the same footprint; the mouse is an 80 g asymmetric ergonomic shell on a MARKSMAN 26K sensor with 140 h on 2.4 GHz and 210 h on Bluetooth (RGB off), six programmable buttons and 100M-click primaries. The genuinely interesting detail is the software posture: all three configure through the browser-based CORSAIR Web Hub with no install, and iCUE desktop support is deferred to end of September. That is CORSAIR deliberately removing the driver from the entry tier — the exact friction point that has dogged G HUB for years, and the one Logitech cannot remove because G HUB is where HITS tuning, rapid trigger and TrueForce profiles live. Availability is muddled (some syndication carries a Jul 28 retail date against an Aug 5 press push), so both cards are dated to the Aug 5 announcement and linked to verified same-day tech-press coverage.

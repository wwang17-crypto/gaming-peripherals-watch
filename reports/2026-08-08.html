The genuinely new signal today is regulatory, not a product. An Aug 6 piece flags that the EU Cyber Resilience Act's incident and vulnerability reporting obligations begin 11 September 2026 — 34 days out — and that they apply to products already on the market, not just new releases. From that date any manufacturer selling connected products into the EU must report an actively exploited vulnerability or severe incident to ENISA within 24 hours, with a 72-hour follow-up and a 14-day final report. The argument for why peripherals are squarely in scope does not rest on the wheel or the mouse: it rests on the Windows companion app that installs a driver or service with elevated privileges, self-updates over the network, and pushes firmware to the device — with the 2021 Razer incident (plugging in a mouse fetched an installer that ran at SYSTEM) as the category's precedent. The realistic near-term impact is low, since the September trigger requires an actual exploited vulnerability; the substance lands 11 December 2027, when security-by-design, documented risk assessment, SBOM, conformity assessment and CE marking apply to every new product placed on the EU market, with penalties up to €15M or 2.5% of global turnover. Worth logging now because this is the one axis where Logitech's G HUB dependency is a compliance surface rather than a UX complaint — G HUB is exactly that class of privileged, self-updating, firmware-pushing software, and the RS50 does not function without it. Note the timing against CORSAIR's Friday move: shipping the SKIFF and HARPOON v2 configurable through a browser with no install is not a CRA play, but it happens to shrink the attack surface the CRA is written about.

Today's takeaways

Thursday returns to 0/0/0/0 after yesterday's single Pwnage Vanta card — but the biggest sim-racing item of the month landed inside the window and it is structural, not a SKU. On Aug 5 CORSAIR announced it has acquired the assets of Trak Racer, the Melbourne cockpit maker founded in 2008 by Matt Sten, who joins CORSAIR as Chief Technology Officer for Sim Solutions; terms were not disclosed. Trak Racer slots directly alongside Fanatec, which CORSAIR bought out of Endor AG's insolvency in September 2024 for roughly $43.7M net of cash. The fit is deliberate and complementary — Fanatec owns the electronics (bases, wheels, pedals, shifters, software) and Trak Racer owns the physical rig (aluminium and tubular cockpits, seats, monitor and peripheral mounts, motion and haptic systems) — which means CORSAIR can now sell an entire simulator, frame and all, as a turnkey platform through a retail and distribution network Trak Racer's mostly direct-to-consumer business never had. It also gives CORSAIR its first real foothold in flight simulation via the TR550, plus the TRZ motorcycle simulator, a BWT Alpine F1 partnership dating to 2020, an Aston Martin Aramco co-branded cockpit, and a Mattel Hot Wheels / Matchbox licence with co-branded product slated from 2027. This is an M&A announcement with no SKU, spec, price or date, so it stays out of Section 1 per the same precedent that excluded Razer's Jul 31 StreamElements asset acquisition — but it is the most consequential competitive development since MOZA's Gran Turismo World Series partnership on Jul 28, and it moves the fight one level up the stack: Logitech now faces a rival that sells the whole rig, while its own sim line still stops at a wheelbase, pedals and a desk clamp.
